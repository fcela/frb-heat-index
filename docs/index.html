Mapping Heat in the U.S. Financial System

A replication of the Federal Reserve's heat map of financial-system vulnerabilities, rebuilt indicator-by-indicator from public data. Forty-six indicators feed fourteen components across three vulnerability categories — every cell below is a quantile in its own history. Blue is subdued, red is acute.

Beyond the 44 indicators in the FEDS Notes version, this implementation includes the two additional indicators from the final journal publication (J. Banking & Finance 81, 2017): total SRISK relative to total assets and significant Granger-causality connections among large financial institutions.

The heat map

Fourteen components × quarters. Colors are quantiles of each component's own historical distribution — a red cell means "hotter than most of this component's history," not an absolute level. Hover any cell for the exact value.

Reading the scale: 0.00 is the lowest vulnerability reading on record for that component (deep blue); 1.00 is the highest (deep red). Low values are information, not errors — gray cells are the ones with no observation.

Aggregate vulnerability index

The mean of the fourteen component quantiles, passed through a kernel CDF once more, so the index lives on 0–1. The 2007–2008 episodes are shaded for reference.

Methodology

Every indicator passes through the same six-step pipeline, documented formula-by-formula in the replication paper (fed_heat_map-replication.pdf).

  1. Sign orientation.

    Each series is flipped as needed so that higher values always mean greater vulnerability. Compressed credit spreads and low volatility read as complacency; high capital ratios, high saving rates, and tightening lending standards read as safer.

  2. Transform.

    Natural log for strictly positive, right-skewed series (spreads, volatility, issuance); a 10-year trailing moving-average detrending for trending ratio-to-GDP series, so the index reacts to the cyclical component; no transform otherwise.

  3. Standardize.

    Z-scores using each indicator's full-sample mean and standard deviation.

  4. Component index.

    Simple average of the standardized indicators within each component.

  5. Heat CDF.

    Each component is mapped to (0, 1) through a Gaussian kernel CDF with Silverman bandwidth, evaluated at each observation. This is what makes every heat-map cell a historical quantile — colors are relative to history, never absolute levels.

  6. Aggregate index.

    The mean of the 14 component quantiles, mapped through its own kernel CDF again → the 0–1 headline index.

Honest caveats (from the replication paper)

  • Commercial real estate is a pipeline discrepancy, not a data gap. The documented pipeline cannot reproduce the paper's CRE component even on the paper's own data — a finding about the paper itself.
  • The maturity gap has no public proxy. The weighted-average maturity gap at commercial banks cannot be rebuilt from public sources; the maturity-transformation component is built from the remaining indicators.
  • Extending the sample moves history. Standardization, the 10-year detrending, and the kernel-CDF bandwidth are all computed over the full available sample, so values for early quarters shift as new quarters are added. The extended map is the same methodology on a longer sample — not point-for-point comparable with the paper's 2015 map.
  • Coverage: 45 of 46 indicators replicated from named public sources — 43 of the 44 FEDS Notes indicators plus both journal-publication additions (SRISK and Granger-causality connectedness); the end-to-end public-proxy panel tracks the paper's published aggregate index at correlation 0.93.
